# SoundGrid Market Research Report v1

**Prepared:** February 24, 2026
**Author:** Clara "Cipher" Rodriguez — Strategic Business Analyst
**Project:** SoundGrid — Music Industry B2B/B2C Marketplace Platform

---

## Executive Summary

SoundGrid operates at the intersection of three high-value, underserved markets: music contract legal tech, sync licensing infrastructure, and touring/live music coordination. Total addressable market across these verticals exceeds $6.2B globally, with a serviceable addressable market of approximately $1.4B for a platform targeting independent artists, producers, and music professionals in the US market. The market is structurally broken — fragmented tools, delayed payments, opaque deal terms, and no single platform unifying the creator-to-commerce workflow. SoundGrid's opportunity is to become the operating layer for independent music professionals.

## 1. Market Landscape Overview

### 1.1 Music Industry Funding Climate (2024–2025)

The capital environment for music tech has shifted from hype to infrastructure:

- **2024:** ~$978M deployed across 110 music tech deals (204 active investors); down sharply from 2023's $10.08B peak but reflecting a flight-to-quality

- **2025:** Over $7B YTD through Q3; Q4 2025 alone surpassed $1B — shaping up as one of the largest years ever for music tech funding

- **Stage mix:** 46% of 2024 deals were pre-seed/seed, signaling strong early-stage conviction

- **Thematic shift:** Capital is flowing away from pure streaming plays toward infrastructure — rights management, contract tooling, live music coordination, and AI-powered creator tools

**Key 2024–2025 funding signals relevant to SoundGrid:**

- ElevenLabs: $180M Series D (AI audio/voice infrastructure)

- Suno: $250M at $2.45B valuation (AI music generation, despite active copyright litigation)

- Concord: $1.76B ABS transaction (catalog monetization infrastructure)

- Chord Music Partners: $2B–$4B catalog deals (institutional music asset validation)

**Implication:** Investors are betting on infrastructure layers. SoundGrid's positioning as the contractual and commercial rails for independent music professionals aligns directly with this thesis.

## 2. Competitive Landscape

### 2.1 Music Contract & Legal Tech

#### Existing Players

**Flou**

- Focus: Simplified music contract creation for independent artists

- Positioning: "Easy mode" contract builder with templates

- Gaps: No payment execution, no negotiation workflow, no sync-specific tooling

- Traction: Early-stage; documented case studies in 2024

**Songpact**

- Focus: Standardized split sheets and collaboration agreements

- Positioning: Free/low-cost entry, standardized templates

- Gaps: No royalty tracking integration, no enforcement, no DAW/DSP integration

- Traction: Growing but niche; lacks enterprise features

**Clearnote** (launched publicly February 2026)

- Focus: Entertainment industry contract drafting platform

- Positioning: AI-assisted drafting for entertainment professionals

- Gaps: Lawyer-facing tool, not artist-facing; high price point for independents

- Traction: Very early; just launched publicly

**Tone**

- Focus: AI-powered contract analyzer for music deals

- Positioning: "Understand what you're signing" — analysis, not creation

- Gaps: Reactive (analyze existing contracts), not proactive; no workflow integration

- Traction: Emerging; 2024–2025 launch window

**Gordy AI / SR2_ / Auracles**

- Focus: Various AI music contract and royalty tools

- Positioning: Niche, vertically specific

- Gaps: Fragmented; no full-stack workflow

**TrackRights**

- Focus: Rights management and ownership tracking

- Positioning: Metadata-first approach

- Gaps: No contract creation, no payment execution

### 2.2 Sync Licensing Infrastructure

#### Market Context

- Global sync licensing market: **$800M+ in 2025**, growing at 7.2% CAGR through 2033

- Independent publishers generated approximately EUR 2.7B in 2023 (IMPALA data)

- Content demand from streaming platforms (Netflix, Amazon, Disney+, Apple TV+) and short-form video (TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts) is accelerating licensing activity

#### Existing Players

**Musicbed**

- Focus: High-quality sync licensing for filmmakers and content creators

- Pricing: Subscription ($16.60–$49.99/mo) + per-license fees

- Gaps: Not artist-facing for contract management; discovery-only model

- Strength: Strong brand with content creators

**Artlist**

- Focus: Unlimited music licensing for video creators

- Pricing: $199–$299/year subscriptions

- Gaps: No artist contract tooling; bulk licensing erodes per-track value

- Strength: 30M+ users; strong distribution

**Epidemic Sound**

- Focus: Subscription sync licensing, B2B enterprise

- Pricing: $15/mo personal to enterprise tiers

- Gaps: Closed network (in-house music only); no indie artist onboarding

- Strength: 40M+ end users via YouTube/TikTok creator integrations

**Soundstripe / Pond5 / Storyblocks**

- Focus: Stock music with sync licenses

- Gaps: Commodity positioning; poor artist economics; no contract layer

**HAAWK / Identifyy**

- Focus: Content ID and royalty tracking

- Gaps: Enforcement only; no deal-making or contract layer

### 2.3 Live Music / Touring Coordination

#### Market Context

- Global live music market: **$31.1B in 2023**, growing at 9.6% CAGR

- Touring tech/coordination software is fragmented across venue booking, routing, ticketing, and logistics

- Independent artists and mid-tier touring acts have virtually no integrated tooling

#### Existing Players

**Sofar Sounds**

- Model: Intimate live events in 400+ cities; artist services; fan data tools

- Revenue: Ticket sales (63–70% to artists + $100 minimum per show), artist services subscriptions, VIP experiences, content licensing

- Gaps: No contract layer; no routing/touring workflow; closed ecosystem

- Traction: Claims $650M in artist-generated ticket/merch sales since pandemic restart

**Bandsintown / Songkick**

- Focus: Tour date discovery and fan notification

- Gaps: No booking, no contracts, no financial infrastructure; pure discovery

- Traction: Millions of users but monetization remains thin

**Gigmit / GigSalad / GigFinder**

- Focus: Booking marketplaces (primarily small gigs, cover bands, private events)

- Gaps: Not designed for touring artists; no contract enforcement; low deal values

- Traction: Niche and fragmented

**Tourlink**

- Focus: Tour management software (itinerary, logistics)

- Gaps: No marketplace, no booking, no contract execution

- Traction: Small, indie-focused

**Booking Agents / Traditional Infrastructure**

- Reality: Mid-tier independent touring artists (earning $50K–$500K annually from live) still largely manage bookings via email, spreadsheets, and verbal agreements

- No platform has solved the routing + contract + payment loop for this segment

## 3. Target Customer Segments

### 3.1 Primary Segments

**Segment A: Independent Artists (Core)**

- Definition: Artists releasing music independently or via distributors (DistroKid, TuneCore, CD Baby); earning $10K–$500K annually from music

- Size: 11M+ independent artists globally; ~3.5M active in the US

- Pain: No contract infrastructure; no sync deal workflow; no touring coordination

- Willingness to Pay: Low-to-moderate; price-sensitive; freemium with upsell

- Key Jobs-to-be-Done: Get paid correctly, protect creative ownership, book gigs without a manager

**Segment B: Music Producers / Beatmakers**

- Definition: Producers creating beats, co-writing, and contributing to recordings; often without formal representation

- Size: ~800K active in the US; highly underserved

- Pain: Unsigned agreements (documented in Travis Scott / Kanye cases); royalty delays; split sheet confusion

- Willingness to Pay: Moderate; directly tied to lost income from poor contract management

- Key Jobs-to-be-Done: Lock in splits before release, get paid when album drops, protect beat ownership

**Segment C: Music Supervisors & Sync Buyers**

- Definition: TV/film/ad music supervisors seeking licensable tracks with clean clearances

- Size: ~8,000–12,000 active in US/UK/EU markets

- Pain: Metadata gaps; unclear ownership; slow clearance workflows

- Willingness to Pay: High; budget authority; subscription + per-placement fee model

- Key Jobs-to-be-Done: Find cleared music fast, execute licenses without legal delay

**Segment D: Venue Operators & Event Coordinators**

- Definition: Independent venue owners, promoters, and event coordinators booking independent talent

- Size: ~45,000 independent venues in the US; ~180,000 event coordinators

- Pain: No standardized contracts; payment disputes; no centralized artist database

- Willingness to Pay: Moderate; operational savings justify SaaS spend

- Key Jobs-to-be-Done: Book artists cleanly, execute contracts, pay without disputes

### 3.2 Secondary / Enterprise Segments

- **Music Publishers (Independent):** Need sync pipeline management and royalty tracking tools

- **Entertainment Attorneys:** White-label contract infrastructure clients; referral network

- **Management Companies:** Portfolio management for artist rosters across bookings, contracts, royalties

## 4. Market Gaps & Pain Points

### 4.1 The Payment Delay Crisis (Documented)

Billboard documented that producers on Travis Scott's *Utopia* (July 2023) had not received signed producer agreements 6+ months after release — preventing any compensation. Over 10 producers on Kanye West's *Vultures* albums faced identical issues. This is systemic, not anecdotal:

- Music is commonly released before contracts are finalized

- No platform currently prevents upload until agreements are executed

- Independent artists have no escrow mechanism for advance payments

- Split sheets are frequently verbal or informal, creating post-release disputes

**SoundGrid Opportunity:** Build "pre-release contract verification" as a distribution gate — music cannot be distributed until splits are signed. This is a platform-native enforcement mechanism no competitor offers.

### 4.2 The Sync Licensing Discovery Gap

- Sync supervisors have no centralized database of cleared, metadata-complete independent tracks

- Independent artists cannot reach supervisors without an agent or publisher

- Custom license negotiation is entirely manual (email, PDF, phone)

- Micro-licensing (TikTok/Reels) generates volume but no infrastructure for custom deals

**SoundGrid Opportunity:** Build the two-sided sync marketplace connecting cleared indie tracks to verified supervisors, with contract generation and payment built in.

### 4.3 The Touring Infrastructure Void

- Independent touring artists earning $50K–$500K annually from live have no integrated booking + contract + payment tool

- Routing (venue sequencing, geography optimization) is done manually

- Performance agreements are frequently unsigned or verbal for smaller dates

- No platform provides post-show settlement tracking

**SoundGrid Opportunity:** Build the routing + booking + contract + settlement layer for the independent touring segment.

### 4.4 The Metadata & Rights Clarity Problem

- Rights ownership is frequently unclear or unregistered at time of sync inquiry

- DSP metadata is inconsistent across platforms

- AI training licensing is an emerging, unresolved compliance need

- Multi-territory rights management remains entirely manual for independents

**SoundGrid Opportunity:** Build metadata verification and rights registry as a platform service — creating a competitive moat through data quality.

## 5. Regulatory & Legal Environment

### 5.1 AI Music Copyright Risk

- Suno is named in active litigation by major publishers despite its $250M funding round

- AI-generated music licensing remains legally unresolved across US and EU jurisdictions

- **Implication:** SoundGrid should position as pro-artist rights infrastructure, not AI music generation — capturing the compliance need as AI music proliferates

### 5.2 Music Modernization Act (MMA) Compliance

- The MMA created mechanical licensing through the Mechanical Licensing Collective (MLC)

- Independent artists remain largely unaware of or unenrolled in MLC

- **Implication:** SoundGrid can offer MLC enrollment assistance and mechanical royalty tracking as a platform-native feature

### 5.3 International Sync Compliance

- Multi-territory sync deals require country-specific licensing structures (PRO registrations, neighboring rights, etc.)

- No indie-facing platform offers automated multi-territory compliance

- **Implication:** Long-term international expansion opportunity; near-term focus on US/UK

## 6. Technology Trends

### 6.1 AI-Powered Contract Intelligence

- Emerging standard: AI contract analysis is becoming table stakes (Clearnote, Tone, Gordy AI all entering 2025–2026)

- **SoundGrid Advantage:** Integrate AI analysis as a feature within a workflow platform, not a standalone tool

- **Moat:** Proprietary dataset of music industry contract terms; AI trained on music-specific deal structures

### 6.2 Blockchain / Smart Contract Settlement

- Early adoption for royalty splits (no dominant player yet)

- Payment execution linked to contract milestones is technically feasible

- **SoundGrid Opportunity:** Build escrow + payment execution as a differentiator; blockchain optional but smart contract logic is viable via traditional payment rails

### 6.3 DAW & Distribution Platform Integration

- The workflow starts in the studio (Pro Tools, Logic, Ableton) and ends at the DSP

- No contract or legal tech platform currently integrates at the DAW or distribution layer

- **SoundGrid Opportunity:** API integrations with DistroKid, TuneCore, CD Baby to trigger contract workflows at distribution

### 6.4 Short-Form Video Licensing Explosion

- TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts driving micro-licensing volume

- Platform licensing deals (TikTok → labels) leave independent artists out

- **SoundGrid Opportunity:** Direct micro-licensing infrastructure for independent tracks on short-form platforms

## 7. Market Entry Strategy

### 7.1 Recommended Beachhead Market

**Target:** US-based independent music producers and artists in the $25K–$250K annual music income range

**Rationale:**

- Highest acute pain (payment delays, no contract infrastructure)

- Moderate willingness to pay when framed as income protection

- Network effects: one producer's adoption pulls in all collaborating artists

- Largest cohort of potential users (~1.2M in the US in this income range)

### 7.2 Go-to-Market Channels

| Channel                                     | Rationale                                           | CAC Estimate       |
| :------------------------------------------ | :-------------------------------------------------- | :----------------- |
| Producer community forums (Reddit, Discord) | Organic reach to highest-pain segment               | Low ($50–$150)     |
| Music attorney referral network             | Trust signal; enterprise upsell path                | Medium ($200–$400) |
| DAW/distribution platform integrations      | Embedded acquisition at workflow moment             | Low ($75–$200)     |
| Sync supervisor partnerships                | B2B supply-side activation                          | Medium ($300–$600) |

### 7.3 Expansion Sequence

1. **Phase 1 (Months 1–12):** Contract creation + split sheets + payment escrow for producers and independent artists

2. **Phase 2 (Months 12–24):** Sync licensing marketplace — connect cleared tracks to verified supervisors

3. **Phase 3 (Months 24–36):** Live music coordination — touring routing, venue booking, performance agreement execution
